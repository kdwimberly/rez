Book I · Prologue

PROLOGUE

Scroll to direct ↓

“What if we fail? The…the rune—it’s never been tested. Not like this.”

A small, middle-aged man with glasses snapped back, his words as brittle as his hair. “The portal will hold. It has to. We’ve worked too hard to back out now, anyway.”

“Quiet, both of you. We are not summoning Him—the portal is simply a path. It will hold.”

The leader enforced control like a shepherd, staff in hand. An unmatched authority.

Below Terra

The cavern where their voices echoed was a gash in the earth, a place where light was scared to go.

Its walls were blackened, scorched by an age-old fire that had long since died, though never forgotten. Striations of molten red pulsed beneath the stone, veins alive with restless energy, thrumming like a heartbeat.

Slow, deep. Eternal.

And yet, no natural flame or heat touched the air here—it was cold. Not quite freezing, though.

Six figures stood at its center, encircling this massive symbol carved into the stone floor.

The rune

Each line pulsed with a violet glow—hypnotic and dreadful.

It was an unnatural geometry, somehow impossibly perfect yet terrible to behold—truly not meant for mortal minds. Where its lines crossed, angles twisted and turned, forming patterns that throbbed with a nauseating rhythm. The combination of glyphs seemed to stare back at those who dared to look at it.

Alive and waiting.

The six summoners—Warlocks—stood in rigid formation, their crimson-black robes pooling like rivers of blood around their feet. These were no Apprentice Glyphwrights or even Specialists. Each bore power etched into their flesh—tattoos of glyphs and runes inked in etheric resin that shimmered when they moved. They were Ascended from the Eclipsed Circle in Umbral Citadel—wielders of knowledge that men should not hold.

And yet, the cavern dwarfed them—swallowing them in her chasm.

Vorthas

At the head of the circle stood Vorthas, the oldest among them and their leader.

Haggard and thin, his skin was like crumpled parchment wrapped tight over bone. The glyphic tattoos etched into his corpse-like body glimmered faintly in the cavern’s pale light, winding like sacred geometry across his hollow cheeks and bald scalp. They marked him not just as a master but as one who had paid dearly for his knowledge. He gripped his staff tightly, his knuckles bloodless, though his gaze remained steady.

To his left stood Tivroy, the scholar—short, thin, with a long ink-black beard and hands forever trembling from years of experimenting with the sacred resin. He adjusted his glasses nervously, their lenses cracked and fogged with condensation from the cavern’s damp chill.

Beside him stood Melara, her fiery hair the only warmth in the cave. Her eyes were veiled behind a cloth woven with glyphs of protection. Her lips pressed into a tight line as she ran her fingers over the silver runes on her dagger’s hilt—a weapon she had vowed to use only if it came to the unspeakable.

Borland the Shattered loomed beside her, his scarred face a testament to countless battles and a betrayal that had almost cost him his life. His broad shoulders were hunched, his body instinctively tensed as though expecting an attack even now, in the cold silence of the cavern.

Kaelin, the youngest of them, stood with his staff planted firmly on the rocky ground, though his sweaty grip showed his relative inexperience. His breath came in shallow gasps as he tried to steady his chunky body, unwilling to let the others see his fear.

And then there was Siris, silent and sharp as a drawn blade. Her dark, mahogany, angular features were illuminated faintly by the rune, though she had positioned herself slightly apart from the others. She was here not because of belief but because she had nothing left to lose.

The chosen

Together, the six of them formed a circle around the rune—a delicate and deadly pattern.

Its edges were filled with a dark, shimmering resin that pulsed faintly as though breathing.

For a moment, no one spoke. The air just lounged lazily, thick with the weight of unspoken thoughts.

Melara tilted her head slightly, as if she could hear something the others couldn’t. “The glyph is… it’s too quiet,” she murmured. “I don’t trust silence this deep.”

Borland grunted, his voice gravelly. “The only thing we need to trust is ourselves. If any one of us falters, the whole ritual collapses. Remember that.” His gaze swept the circle, lingering on Kaelin for a heartbeat longer than the others.

Vorthas raised a hand, silencing them. His voice carried an authority that needed no volume. “There is no room for doubt. None. Whatever fears you have, bury them. What we call forth will not care for your hesitations or regrets. It will answer only to strength—and sacrifice.”

The word sacrifice hung in the air like frost, chilling them all.

Kaelin swallowed hard, his fingers tightening around his staff. Tivroy adjusted his glasses again, mumbling something under his breath.

Siris finally spoke, her voice cold and low. “We were chosen for this. Not because we’re ready, but because no one else can do this. So, stop looking for ways out and do what you came here to do.”

Vorthas nodded approvingly, though his expression remained grim. “Siris is right. This is not a time for hesitation. It is a time for action. We have prepared for this moment for years. We will see it through.”

He raised his hands over the rune, weathered fingers trembling slightly.

“No more delays.”

The others straightened, their postures stiffening as they prepared themselves.

They closed their eyes, allowing the silence to deepen their focus.

“Rii’arvaal ekmor,” Vorthas intoned, the words slicing through the air like a blade.

The chant began, low and steady, their voices weaving together into a deep, guttural symphony. It was more than a sound; it was a vibration that seeped into the stone, into their bones, into the very air they breathed.

As their voices rose in unison, the rune flared brighter, its light spreading outward in tendrils that shimmered like liquid fire.

And still, the silence within the cavern seemed to press inward, cold and suffocating, as if the air began to hold its breath for what was to come.

Above them, the void shook.

It began as a fissure, a crack in the ceiling of darkness. From within that gaping maw, shadows twisted, not just as the absence of light, no, but as something with shape and hunger—with density. A vortex churned there, spiraling outward, shreds of darkness coiling in toward the center like a storm of ink.

“Keep the formation! Do not falter! Focus!”

Vorthas barked, jaw clenched, sweat beading on his brow.

Tivroy’s trembling hands clenched into fists. “It’s—it’s answering us!” he whispered, his voice tinged with absolute awe and terror, eyes wide open.

First response

The molten veins in the walls glowed brighter. Fissures cracked and splintered outward.

The others pressed on, their chanting loud and furious. The portal grew. Wind tore through the cavern, tugging at their cloaks, pulling their voices into the void. Dust and small stones spiraled upward, as though gravity itself no longer applied.

And then He spoke.

The sound hit them like a physical force. It was not a voice but a weight—a roar of meaning that bypassed the ear and reason alike, sinking fangs into their souls.

It came from within.

They staggered as one. Melara screamed, blood trickling from beneath her cloth-blindfold as her warded vision shattered. Borland dropped to a knee, clutching his head. “He…He knows me…” he rasped, trembling.

Above them, the vortex began to swirl violently. Shapes moved in the dark—things with eyes that gleamed like embers and elongated claws that scratched against unseen barriers.

And deeper still, beyond those shadows, a shape emerged: the silhouette of something vast, colossal, draped in a mantle of fire and black smoke. Its form towered, proportions wrong, its presence so immense that it seemed to press against the cavern’s walls.

A crown of flames hung above His shadowed head—flames that did not rise, but fell, cascading downward like liquid fire unraveling toward the void. No eyes, no mouth—just a single, searing slit of light, burning straight down His face like an open wound tearing reality itself.

And it was looking at them.

Beyond the threshold

And it was looking at them.

Gravity surrendered. Stone rose. The cavern began to come apart.

“It’s reversed! It’s pulling us in! Vorthas! VORTHAS, IT’S PULLING US IN!”

Tivroy’s scream tore through the chaos, though barely audible.

The vortex had turned hungry, insatiable, the suction pulling like invisible chains. It yanked at their drapes, tearing at the fabric, bending the air itself toward the churning dark.

Vorthas’ face twisted in panic. “Anchor yourselves! Seal it off!”

Borland dropped to both knees, his arms flaring with violet glyphs as he clawed at the ground. “STEADY! STEADY! HOLD FAST!” The symbols ignited with desperate power, casting a purple glow across his skin, but the vortex pulled harder. Blood burst from his nose and ears as the glyphs overloaded.

“It’s freezing—why is it freezing?” Melara wailed. The wind should have burned with the firestorm above, yet the air was like the coldest winter. The kind of cold that sank into bone, reaching depths of marrow, whispering promises of things worse than eternal suffering.

Flames within the portal itself curled downward like tentacles. Bitter, blue fire drained the light from the room. It was as though the abyss had swallowed the sun and spat out an expanse of nothingness.

“Kaelin, NO!”

Tivroy’s voice cracked as Kaelin broke formation.

His staff slipped from his hands as he stumbled backward, his breath hitching in ragged panic. “No—no, it’s wrong, it’s pulling—”

He turned and ran, crying out as though distance alone could save him. For a heartbeat, he believed it—he actually believed he’d escaped the circle. His foot crossed the outer ring. Safety. Freedom.

Then the shadows noticed him.

They unfurled across the stone like ink chasing spilled water, coils of darkness rising to hook around his ankles. Kaelin screamed, scrabbling at the floor as the tendrils tightened. His nails split. Blood streaked the stone. The shadows pulled.

His spine arched with a crack like splintering wood—a snapped bow. His mouth hung open in a silent scream as his body folded in on itself—once, twice—before the darkness yanked him backward and devoured him whole.

Borland roared in defiance, planting his feet, his muscles flexing with impossible strength. Glyphs shimmered along his chest and forearms as he thrust his blade into the floor, using it to anchor himself. The suction pulled at him—his body rose a few inches off the ground, his grip the only thing keeping him rooted.

“You are nothing without us!”

The blade glowed violet-hot, resisting the pull of the vortex, but then the shadows surged again—like a wave of ink swallowing the light. Borland’s arms trembled, his voice turning to a howl as the metal warped, the heat freezing into a jagged edge. The sword shattered, and Borland was gone—ripped into the dark with a sound like wet fabric shredding.

Melara wept openly, her pale hands clawing at her blindfold as her glyphs burned against her skin. “I will not feed your hunger,” she whispered, her voice raw. “I will not—”

Gone. The portal had decided otherwise.

One by one, the Warlocks fell—consumed not by fire but by absence, by cold so complete it felt like the unraveling of existence itself. As Siris’ broken body tumbled through the air, her final scream echoed in the cavern—cut short as the vortex swallowed her whole.

His legs shook, his arms outstretched as he poured every ounce of his will into the glyphs and runes embodying him. His voice quaked as he whispered words no longer filled with power but with hopeless despair.

Vorthas’ eyes widened, horror blooming where certainty once lived. “I didn’t… this is not…” he faltered, stumbling over his words.

“I have misjudged…Your dominion.”

The void answered him with silence. The shadows came. Cold followed.

And Vorthas was taken—devoured.

The last Warlock

The shadows came. Cold followed.

The cavern was quiet again

And Vorthas was taken—devoured.

The cavern was quiet again, except for the faint hum of the portal—still open. The rune’s light dimmed, then died, its tendrils retreating as though the earth itself exhaled. Above, the vortex swirled, and the faint outline of something vast remained beyond the depths, waiting.

Then, a sound. Not a whisper—a screech.

Horrific. Inhuman. A sound of absolute primal terror, of consciousness being torn apart and reconstructed in the same terrible moment. The sound of something being dismembered between worlds, ripping itself into existence through membranes of reality that were never meant to be breached.

The portal’s edges began to pulse—to breathe. In and out.

In and out.

The air thickened, heavy with the scent of decay laced with something sweetly rotten, like fruit left out too long. Beneath the silence, there was a slow, wet sound—steady.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

It was as if the very fabric of the world was beginning to bleed.

A low, resonant hum rose from the depths of the vortex, swelling into a tremor that shook the cavern walls. It crept into every corner of the space, weaving through the stone like a whispered promise of dominion.

The rift throbbed once more, an inhale that seemed to draw the cavern itself closer. Then, a chilling, high-pitched wail cried out toward eternity, followed by a deafening silence.

Bells tolled, breaking the lull, a slow rhythm, coming from deep within the portal.

Aftermath

Bells tolled, breaking the lull—a slow rhythm from deep within the portal.

Silence.

HIS KINGDOM
IS CALLING.

End of Prologue
